Does your business really need a website?

Harsh Dubey · 24 November 2026 · 4 min read

In this article

  2. When you can wait
  3. What a website does that social media can’t
  4. What a small-business website actually needs

  6. How to start without overspending

Plenty of good businesses run on referrals, a WhatsApp number and a Google Maps pin. So it’s a fair question: do you actually need a website, or is it one more thing to pay for and forget about?

Here is a simple way to decide, the honest cases where you can wait, and what a website has to do to be worth having.

The quick test

You need a website if any of these are true:

  • People search for what you do before they call. If someone types “physiotherapist near me” or “corporate gift hampers in Pune”, you want to be one of the answers.
  • You get enquiries from people who don’t already know you. Strangers want to check you’re real before they get in touch.
  • You sell to other businesses. A buyer at a company will look you up, and often has to show a colleague who you are.
  • Your competitors have one. When a customer compares three options, the one with no website usually loses by default.
  • You answer the same questions every day. Prices, timings, location, what’s included. A website can answer them before the phone rings.

If none of these apply, you may genuinely be fine without one for now. That’s the next section.

When you can wait

A website is not always the first thing to spend on. You can reasonably hold off if:

  • All your work comes from referrals and you’re already turning people away.
  • Your customers find you walking past, and almost nobody searches for you online.
  • You haven’t yet decided exactly what you sell or to whom. A website built around a guess usually gets rebuilt.

In those cases, two free steps cover most of the gap: a complete Google Business Profile (hours, photos, services, phone number) and a WhatsApp Business profile with a short catalogue. Do those properly and revisit the question in a few months.

What a website does that social media can’t

An Instagram page or a Maps listing is useful, but it isn’t yours, and it isn’t built to turn a visitor into an enquiry. A website is different in a few practical ways:

  • You own it. Platforms change their rules, reach and layout. Your domain and your site stay put.
  • It shows up for what you do, not just your name. A page about a service can appear when people search for that service.
  • It answers questions in order. What you do, who it’s for, what it costs, how to start. Social feeds show your latest post, not your best explanation.
  • It makes the next step obvious. A call button, a WhatsApp button or a short form, right where someone decides.
  • It reassures bigger customers. Companies, institutions and overseas clients expect to find a proper website before they deal with you.

What a small-business website actually needs

It doesn’t need to be big. It needs to do six things well:

  1. Say what you do, for whom and where, in the first screen. If a visitor has to scroll to understand it, many won’t.
  2. List your services clearly, with prices or a starting price where you can. “Contact us for pricing” on everything puts people off.
  3. Show real proof. Photos of your actual work, premises or team, and a link to your Google reviews.
  4. Give one obvious way to get in touch: call, WhatsApp or a short form, visible on every page.
  5. Work properly on a phone. Most of your visitors will be on one.
  6. Load quickly, including on mobile data.

That can be a single, well-made page. More pages make sense once you know which services people ask about most.

What it doesn’t need on day one

  • Dozens of pages written to fill space.
  • Heavy animations and sliders that slow it down.
  • A blog, unless you’ll actually write for it.
  • Every feature a website builder offers.

A clear one-page site that answers real questions beats a ten-page site nobody reads.

How to start without overspending

  1. Register the domain in your own name, with an email address you control. This is the most common thing small businesses lose.
  2. Start with one page that covers the six points above.
  3. Link it to your Google Business Profile and your WhatsApp.
  4. Choose a platform you can update yourself, so changing a price or adding a photo doesn’t need a developer.
  5. Add pages based on real questions once people start using it.

Whoever builds it, make sure you end up with the logins for the domain, the hosting and the site itself. A website you can’t access isn’t really yours.

Talk about your website

Frequently asked questions

Is an Instagram page enough instead of a website?

It can be a good start, especially for visual businesses. But you don’t own the page, it won’t show up for most service searches, and it isn’t built to turn a visitor into an enquiry. Many businesses use both: social media for reach, a website for the decision.

How much does a small-business website cost?

It depends on how many pages you need, whether you want to sell online, and whether you’ll update it yourself. A focused one-page site costs far less than an online store. We scope and price every project in writing before starting, so you know the cost up front.

Can I update the website myself?

Yes, if it’s built on a platform designed for that, such as WordPress or Shopify, or with a simple editor. Ask for this at the start: changing prices, photos and opening hours shouldn’t need a developer.
