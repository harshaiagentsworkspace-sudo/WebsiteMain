FAQ

Frequently asked questions

01

What is the difference between a chatbot and an AI agent?

A chatbot answers questions in a conversation. An AI agent carries out a task, such as sorting an enquiry, updating a record or preparing a reply, using the tools it has been connected to.

02

Can an agent work with our existing software?

Usually, yes. Agents connect to tools that offer an API or a supported integration, such as many CRMs, inboxes, forms and spreadsheets. We check your tools before we propose anything.

03

Can we control what the agent is allowed to do?

Yes. You decide its limits, which actions need a person’s approval and what it must always hand over. Those rules are part of the written scope.

04

Is our business data safe?

The agent gets only the access it needs, and we tell you which AI provider processes your data and under what terms before anything is connected. Sensitive actions can always require approval.

05

Do we need an AI agent or a simpler automation?

If the steps are the same every time, a simpler automation is usually the better choice. We’ll tell you which fits after looking at the work itself.

06

What happens if the agent gets something wrong?

Because sensitive actions wait for approval, most mistakes are caught before they reach a customer. Every action is logged, so an error can be traced and the agent’s instructions or limits adjusted. We test on real past cases before launch to find weak spots early.

In more detail

For those who like the full picture

What an AI agent is

An AI agent is software that uses a language model to work through a task: it reads what has come in, decides what kind of case it is, and takes the next step using the tools it has been given, such as looking up a record, drafting a reply or updating your CRM.

Unlike a chatbot, an agent doesn’t just answer questions. It does a piece of work, and it can be set up to stop and ask a person whenever it reaches a decision it shouldn’t make alone.

Automation or an agent: which do you need?

If the steps are the same every time, a workflow automation is simpler, cheaper and more predictable. An agent earns its place when each case is a little different and needs reading and judgement before anything happens.

  • Automation: the same input leads to the same steps, every time
  • Agent: the input varies, and the right next step depends on what it says
  • Often the answer is both: an automation hands the unclear cases to an agent, and the agent hands the hard ones to a person

Business applications

Good first projects are tasks with plenty of volume, a clear definition of a good result and a person who can review the output.

  • Reading and sorting incoming enquiries and support requests
  • Drafting replies and quotes from your own information for approval
  • Checking documents or forms against a list of requirements
  • Keeping CRM records and spreadsheets up to date
  • Preparing research summaries and briefings for your team

Tool integration

An agent is only as useful as the tools it can use. We connect agents to your systems through their APIs or supported integrations, such as your CRM, helpdesk, inbox, calendar, spreadsheets or document store, and give each one a defined set of actions: look up a record, draft a reply, create a task, update a field.

Approval controls

You decide which actions an agent may take on its own and which need a person’s sign-off. Typical rules: anything sent to a customer, any price or commitment, and any change to financial or personal records waits for approval. The agent prepares the work; a person confirms it.

Security considerations

An agent gets only the access its job needs, with its own credentials where the tool allows. Before anything is connected, we explain which AI provider processes your data and under what terms, avoid sending data the task doesn’t need, and keep a log of every action the agent takes.

Monitoring and maintenance

After launch, we review the agent’s logs and outputs with you, look at the cases it handed to a person, and refine its instructions, information and limits. AI models and the tools they connect to change over time, so agents need periodic checks to keep working as intended.
