FAQ

Frequently asked questions

01

What kind of business is AI automation suitable for?

Any business where the same manual steps repeat every day: answering similar enquiries, chasing leads, copying details between tools or preparing the same report each week. Small teams often feel the benefit most, because those hours come out of a few people’s days.

02

Do we need technical knowledge to use it?

No. We build and connect the workflow, test it with you and hand over plain notes on how it works. Your team keeps using the tools they already know.

03

Will automation replace our staff?

That isn’t the aim. Automation takes on the repetitive steps so your team can spend more time on customers and on work that needs judgement.

04

What happens if an automation makes a mistake?

Steps that matter wait for a person to approve them, every run is logged, and a failed step stops the workflow and alerts someone. We also test on real past cases before going live.

05

How is this different from AI agent development?

Automation follows the steps you define, with AI helping on parts such as reading or drafting. An AI agent decides how to handle each case within limits you set. Many projects start with automation; we suggest an agent only when the work needs it.

06

How is AI automation different from workflow automation?

Our Workflow Automation service connects tools and automates rule-based steps. AI Automation adds AI models for the steps that involve reading, interpreting or drafting text. Many projects combine both.

07

How long does it take to automate a workflow?

It depends on the number of steps and tools involved. One well-defined workflow is a much smaller project than a process that spans several systems. We give you a timeline in the written scope once we have mapped the work.

In more detail

For those who like the full picture

What AI adds to automation

Traditional automation moves information along fixed paths. AI models add the ability to read and interpret: understanding what an email is asking, pulling details out of a document, summarising a long thread or drafting a reply in your tone. Combined, they can handle processes that used to need a person at every step.

Which work is worth automating

The best candidates are tasks your team does often, the same way each time, and that mostly move information from one place to another. If a task needs real judgement on every case, it may suit an AI agent with a person approving, or it may be better left as it is.

  • It happens every day or every week
  • It follows roughly the same steps each time
  • It means copying details between tools, inboxes or spreadsheets
  • Delays cost you something: a slow reply, a missed follow-up, a late report

Benefits and limitations

Done well, automation reduces time spent on routine tasks, makes responses more consistent and keeps information up to date across your tools. How much it helps depends on how often a task happens and how much of it follows a pattern, so we estimate it from your process rather than promising a figure.

AI has limits too. Language models can misread an unusual request or state something incorrect with confidence. That is why we test on real cases, ground answers in your own information, and keep a person approving anything that commits your business.

  • Good fit: frequent, repetitive tasks with clear inputs
  • Good fit: work that is mostly reading, sorting or moving information
  • Poor fit: decisions that need expert judgement on every case
  • Poor fit: processes that change completely from one week to the next

Practical examples

Typical applications, described as possibilities rather than past client results:

  • A website enquiry is categorised, logged in your CRM and a draft reply is prepared for your approval
  • New leads are checked against your criteria and the strongest are sent to the right person
  • Order or invoice details are extracted from emails and entered into a spreadsheet or accounting tool
  • A weekly summary of enquiries, sales or support requests is assembled and emailed on a set day
  • Customer questions are answered from your published policies, with anything unusual passed to a person

Human oversight: people stay in control

An automation should make your team faster, not hide what is happening. We decide with you which steps run on their own and which wait for a person to approve, and we keep a record of what each workflow did.

Before anything goes live, we test it on real past cases. If a step fails, the workflow stops and tells someone, rather than carrying on with bad information.

Working with the tools you already use

We connect the CRM, inbox, forms and spreadsheets you already have, wherever they offer a supported connection, instead of asking you to switch. Where a workflow uses a paid third-party tool or an AI service, we tell you the running cost before we build it.

How we implement it

We start small: one workflow, mapped with the people who do the work today. Once the design is agreed in writing, we build it, test it on real past cases and, where it makes sense, run it alongside the manual process for a short period before switching over. You get documentation, and after launch we review how it performs and adjust.
